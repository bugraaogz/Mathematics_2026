## Question

Find the projection of $u=(4,2)$ onto the direction $v=(1,1)$. Then compute $r=u-\mathrm{proj}_v\,u$ and verify that $r\cdot v=0$. Explain this result geometrically.

## Solution

1. Projection of $u$ onto $v$:
$$u \cdot v = 4(1) + 2(1) = 6,\qquad \|v\|^2 = 1^2 + 1^2 = 2$$
$$\mathrm{proj}_v u = \frac{u \cdot v}{\|v\|^2} v = \frac{6}{2} (1,1) = (3,3)$$

2. Perpendicular component $r$:
$$r = u - \mathrm{proj}_v u = (4,2) - (3,3) = (1,-1)$$

3. Verification of orthogonality:
$$r \cdot v = 1(1) + (-1)(1) = 1 - 1 = 0$$

4. Geometrically, vector $u$ is decomposed into two orthogonal components: $\mathrm{proj}_v u$ (parallel to direction $v$) and $r$ (perpendicular to direction $v$).
